## 1. Favourite-longshot bias (FLB) in tennis: magnitude and direction by odds band, ATP vs WTA, main tour vs Challenger/ITF

### Takeaway
Tennis match-winner markets consistently show a classic (positive) favourite-longshot bias: longshots are overpriced and absorb most of the margin. Lahvička found it is **stronger in matches between lower-ranked players, in later rounds and at high-profile tournaments**, a pattern consistent with bookmakers protecting themselves against insiders. The bias is real but, after margin and after correcting for data-snooping, it is generally **not large enough to make blindly backing favourites profitable**. For a price-first bettor the practical point is to be very sceptical of "value" on longshots and to use a de-margining method that accounts for the bias (Shin, or better).

### Cited Findings
- **[STRONG]** Forrest & McHale (2007), *European Journal of Finance* 13(8):751-768: a large dataset of professional men's tennis matches and a new econometric approach find **positive longshot bias throughout the range of odds**, i.e. expected returns fall as odds lengthen at every price level. The paper discusses bettors' attitudes to risk and skewness as the cause. — [Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/13518470701705736); [Salford repository](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/output/1446019/anyone-for-tennis-betting); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/eurjfi/v13y2007i8p751-768.html)
- **[STRONG]** Lahvička (2014), "What causes the favourite-longshot bias? Further evidence from tennis" (*Applied Economics Letters*; MPRA WP 47905): uses **almost 45,000 professional singles matches**. The FLB is **much stronger (i) in matches between lower-ranked players, (ii) in later-round matches, (iii) at high-profile tournaments**. These patterns cannot be explained only by bettors being risk-loving or misperceiving probabilities. They are **consistent with bookmakers protecting themselves against better-informed insiders and against the public exploiting new information**. — [MPRA PDF](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/47905/1/MPRA_paper_47905.pdf); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2287335); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/262963423_What_causes_the_favourite-longshot_bias_Further_evidence_from_tennis)
- **[STRONG]** Abinzano, Muga & Santamaria (2016), *Applied Economics Letters* 23(8), "Game, set and match: the favourite-long shot bias in tennis betting exchanges": the FLB **also exists on betting exchanges** (order-driven, with no bookmaker setting prices) and shows the same pattern: stronger for lower-ranked players, later rounds and high-profile tournaments. — [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13504851.2015.1093074); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2664708)
- **[STRONG]** Abinzano, Muga & Santamaria (2019), *Journal of Sports Economics* 20(2):261-285, "Hidden Power of Trading Activity": on tennis exchanges, odds mispricing (FLB) is **positively associated with trading volume**, **negatively associated with the presence of institutional (informed) bettors**, and **positively related to market uncertainty**. The relative attention paid to the favourite versus the longshot is also positively associated with the FLB. — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1527002517731875); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2788098)
- **Conflict to note:** one generic secondary source claims "the FLB exists only in bookmaker markets; in betting exchanges it does not exist". This is **contradicted by** Abinzano et al. (2016, 2019), who document the FLB on tennis exchanges. — secondary claim appeared in search summary from [The Wager Theorem](https://thewagertheorem.com/favorite-longshot-bias-betting/); contradicted by [Abinzano et al. 2016](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13504851.2015.1093074)
- **[STRONG]** Candila & Scognamillo, "On the longshot bias in tennis betting markets: the CaSco normalization" (Salerno DiSES WP 3_236): uses **Bet365 odds for over 27,000 matches, 2005-2015**. **When there is a clear underdog, the bookmaker increases the bias in the published odds** to limit losses on upsets. Normalisation methods that model the bias do better. Their CaSco method **beat Basic, regression and Shin normalisations in Diebold-Mariano tests (null of equal predictive ability always rejected)**. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/paper/sepwpaper/3_5f236.htm); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/350410131_On_the_longshot_bias_in_tennis_betting_markets_the_CASCO_normalization); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/On-the-Longshot-Bias-in-Tennis-Betting-Markets:-The-Candila-Scognamillo/4fd26b7db40ea1b011470ee432ba99f3d7a12223)
- **[STRONG]** Whelan (2024), "Comparing two methods for testing the efficiency of sports betting markets", *Sports Economics Review*: the regression on normalised probabilities gives valid efficiency tests, while the inverse-odds method is biased against finding the FLB. In large tennis and soccer datasets, **the pattern of low average payouts on longshots is somewhat larger in tennis than in soccer**. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2773161824000193); [Whelan PDF](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/SER.pdf); [CEPR DP19486](https://cepr.org/publications/dp19486)
- **[STRONG]** Lyócsa & Výrost (2018), "To bet or not to bet: a reality check for tennis betting market efficiency", *Applied Economics* 50(20):2251-2272. They test **40 betting rules**. Individual tests suggest some rules earn positive returns, but **once data-snooping bias is controlled (reality-check tests) the evidence largely disappears**, with very little evidence of return differences between rules. Their conclusion: tennis betting markets are "much more efficient than suggested by previous studies". — [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00036846.2017.1394973); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/320672516_To_bet_or_not_to_bet_a_reality_check_for_tennis_betting_market_efficiency)
- **[MODERATE]** A Pinnacle article on the FLB in ATP tennis ("What 40,000 matches reveal") says the bias is "fully alive": the market **protects favourites with thinner margins while longshots absorb most of the overround**. Exact per-band figures could not be retrieved. — [Pinnacle](https://www.pinnacle.com/betting-resources/en/predictions/tennis)
- ATP vs WTA split: no source retrieved gives separate FLB magnitudes for ATP and WTA. Lahvička's dataset covers professional singles, but the gender split was not visible in the snippets. — see Gaps.

### Inferences
- A price-first bettor who de-margins Pinnacle with Shin is already partly correcting for the FLB, since Shin's model assumes insider trading, which is exactly the mechanism Lahvička's evidence supports. CaSco evidence suggests that **Shin may still under-correct in lopsided matches** (at least for Bet365). Apparent "value" on big underdogs at soft books is the most likely to be an artefact of the de-margining method.
- The FLB is strongest in lower-ranked (Challenger/ITF-type) matches. Soft books therefore likely load **extra margin onto underdogs** in exactly the tiers the bettor targets. Signals of "value" on outsiders at those levels deserve a higher EV threshold or rejection. The current odds band of 1.40-4.00 already excludes the deepest longshots.
- No evidence was found that blindly backing favourites beats the margin at a single bookmaker. The FLB is a **pricing-method issue** (how to compute the fair price), not a stand-alone strategy.

### Gaps
- Per-odds-band ROI tables (e.g. return at 1.10-1.30 vs 3.0-5.0) from Forrest & McHale or Lahvička could not be read (full text blocked).
- No study found that directly compares FLB magnitude **ATP vs WTA** or **Challenger vs ITF vs tour** with modern (2020-2026) Pinnacle data.
- No peer-reviewed 2020-2026 update of tennis FLB using Pinnacle closing odds was found.

## 2. Market efficiency differences by segment: WTA vs ATP, Challenger/ITF vs tour, rounds, qualifiers, injury returns, surface, indoor/outdoor, Asian swing, motivation, defending points

### Takeaway
Only a few segment effects have real quantitative backing. The FLB is stronger in lower-ranked, later-round and high-profile matches. Lower tiers have thinner liquidity and less information, which cuts both ways: softer prices but higher insider and fixing risk. Home advantage exists for men but is small. A "buzz"/attention effect in WTA was claimed and then largely overturned. Most popular angles have **no quantified betting-market evidence** and should be treated as folklore: Asian swing jet lag, end-of-season motivation, defending points, surface transitions, indoor/outdoor, qualifiers "in rhythm", and players returning from injury. The consensus of modern ML studies is that **bookmaker odds already contain almost all available information**.

### Cited Findings
- **[STRONG] Market already embeds most information:** Wilkens (2021), *Journal of Sports Analytics*, uses **25,204 ATP and 13,755 WTA matches** with many ML methods. Average prediction accuracy **cannot be pushed above about 70%**. "Most of the relevant information is embedded in the betting markets", and adding player/match data brings no significant improvement. Betting returns are **highly volatile and mainly negative over the longer term**. — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/JSA-200463); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3506302)
- **[STRONG]** Kovalchik (2016), *Journal of Quantitative Analysis in Sports* 12(3):127-138, "Searching for the GOAT of tennis win prediction": tested 11 published models on **2,395 ATP matches (2014)**. The **Bookmaker Consensus Model was most accurate (72%)**, followed by FiveThirtyEight-style Elo (70%). — [VU PDF](https://vuir.vu.edu.au/34652/1/jqas-2015-0059.pdf); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/301577517_Searching_for_the_GOAT_of_tennis_win_prediction)
- **[MODERATE]** Penn, Michael & Bhatt (2026), *Royal Society Open Science*: bookmakers' odds "consistently provide one of the most accurate methods" for tennis. Odds are well approximated by an Elo-style, surface-dependent rating model. — [arXiv 2508.15956](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15956); [RSOS](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rsos/article/13/5/252512/481935/Dynamic-graph-based-forecasts-of-bookmakers-odds)
- **[STRONG] Line shopping is where profit appears:** Angelini, Candila & De Angelis (2022), *EJOR* 297(1):120-132, "Weighted Elo rating for tennis match predictions": **over 60,000 ATP and WTA matches**. WElo beats standard Elo, Bradley-Terry and regression models. However, **ROI is negative when betting at a single bookmaker (Bet365)**. Positive returns depend mainly on **taking the best odds across many bookmakers**. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0377221721003234); [Unibo PDF](https://cris.unibo.it/bitstream/11585/821483/2/Weighted%20ELO%20rating%20predictions%20in%20tennis.pdf)
- **[WEAK, overturned] WTA "buzz" mispricing:** Ramirez, Reade & Singleton (2023), *IJF* 39(3):1413-1423, built a pre-match "buzz" factor from Wikipedia page views and reported **ROI of 17%-29% at Bet365 on over 5,000 potential WTA bets (2019-Mar 2020)**. A **correction study** found the results were driven largely by **a single outlier bet with erroneously long odds**. After fixing the data, **only one strategy (betting on "competitive" matches) remained significantly profitable**. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0169207022001091); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4060984); correction: [arXiv 2306.01740](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01740)
- **[WEAK] Intransitive matchups:** a 2025 arXiv preprint (graph neural network) claims **Pinnacle poorly handles matches with high intransitive complexity** (A beats B, B beats C, C beats A). Betting only such matchups gave **+3.26% ROI with Kelly staking over 1,903 bets** (model accuracy 65.7%, Brier 0.215). It is not peer-reviewed, the sample is small, and statistical significance is not established in the snippet. — [arXiv 2510.20454](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20454)
- **[STRONG] Rounds/profile/ranking:** the FLB is stronger in later rounds, at high-profile events and among lower-ranked players (Lahvička 2014; Abinzano et al. 2016). This is the best-evidenced "segment" effect. — [MPRA](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/47905/1/MPRA_paper_47905.pdf); [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13504851.2015.1093074)
- **[MODERATE] Lower tiers less efficient (practitioner analysis):** Buchdahl (via Pinnacle / Sports Trading Network) analysed **Pinnacle opening and closing odds for ATP, WTA, Challenger and ITF Futures, 12 May 2015-19 Jul 2019: 68,361 matches, 136,722 prices** (data supplied by Miguel Figueres of WinnerOdds, with walkovers and first-set retirements removed). His conclusion: the tennis market "may not be wholly efficient, at least not as efficient as soccer". The FLB tests found evidence of inefficiency. Suggested reasons are **lower liquidity** and the inclusion of **Challenger and Futures events, where far less information is available**. — [Pinnacle](https://www.pinnacle.com/betting-resources/en/tennis/how-efficient-is-the-tennis-betting-market/u522zlsepqja8q2n); [Sports Trading Network mirror](https://www.sportstradingnetwork.com/article/how-efficient-is-the-tennis-betting-market/); related: [Pinnacle, ATP efficiency](https://www.pinnacle.com/betting-resources/en/Tennis/how-efficient-is-the-atp-tennis-betting-market/URU25K89X26GMH62)
- **[WEAK/opinion]** Some practitioner pages claim that "ATP 250 and WTA 250 events are the least efficient tier" and that "Challenger and ITF have the softest pricing but the lowest liquidity and least information". These appeared in search summaries without a clear primary author. Treat them as opinion. — appeared alongside [Tennisnerd betting](https://www.tennisnerd.net/betting) in results
- **[MODERATE] WTA vs ATP predictability:** Sackmann (Tennis Abstract) documented "The Steadily Less Predictable WTA" in 2017; in men's matches the favourite historically wins more often. **Conflicting recent journalism** (2026) argues WTA top players are now more consistent than ATP at Slams/1000s. Neither source measures *betting-market* efficiency; lower predictability is not the same as mispricing. — [Heavy Topspin 2017](https://www.tennisabstract.com/blog/2017/05/28/the-steadily-less-predictable-wta/); contrasted by [Yardbarker 2026](https://www.yardbarker.com/tennis/articles/the_wta_is_now_more_consistent_than_the_atp_and_the_us_open_numbers_prove_it/s1_17460_44266742)
- **[STRONG, small effect] Home advantage:** Koning (2011), *Journal of Sports Sciences* 29(1): a **home advantage exists among male professionals but not among females**. One search summary gave the effect as about 2-3% more matches won than expected from rankings; that exact figure is unverified. A 2025 *JEBO* paper ("Individual performance and environment: Home advantage in ATP tennis") studies how talent (top 10), age and culture moderate home advantage and uses COVID no-crowd matches to isolate crowd effects (numbers not retrieved). — [Koning, doi](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2010.516762); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21104519/); [JEBO 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167268125003555)
- **[MODERATE, no betting link] Jet lag/travel:** Maynard et al. (2026), *Sports Health*: **52 female professional players** wore WHOOP 3.0 while travelling. Crossing time zones produced **circadian desynchronisation, sleep disruption, physiological changes and decreased athletic performance**. It gives no win-probability effect size and no market-pricing test. — [SAGE doi](https://doi.org/10.1177/19417381251387717); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41272436/)
- **[WEAK] Return from injury/layoff:** practitioner guidance only. Rankings can be "20+ spots too optimistic" for a player's first tournaments back, and surface-specific and last-4-6-week form are better indicators. No quantified study was found. — [odds.guru](https://www.odds.guru/learn/tennis/tennis-stats-that-predict-outcomes)
- **[WEAK] Qualifiers:** no study comparing qualifiers' or lucky losers' main-draw win rates with market prices was found. One source notes the structural load of three qualifying matches. — [globaltennisnews](https://www.globaltennisnews.com/how-tennis-qualifying-works/)
- **[FOLKLORE] End-of-season motivation / defending points / tanking:** the only quantitative item found is Rodenberg & Feustel (2014). Using effort proxies, they find **players exert less effort in first-round matches**, where wagering-induced manipulation is more likely, even after adjusting for skill. No study of "defending points" or end-of-season motivation versus odds was found. Only **1.8% of male and 3.1% of female players on the international circuit earn a profit** (context for low-tier financial incentives). — [Rodenberg & Feustel, J. Prediction Markets](https://www.ubplj.org/index.php/jpm/article/view/866); [PLOS One 2024 game-statistics study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11537396/)
- **[FOLKLORE] Surface transitions, indoor vs outdoor, Asian swing heat/humidity:** no betting-market study with effect sizes was found. Surface-dependent ratings are already built into how bookmaker odds behave, per Penn et al. 2026. — [arXiv 2508.15956](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15956)

### Inferences
- The strongest actionable evidence **supports the method already chosen**. The information edge is small, and the robust source of profit is **taking prices above a sharp consensus**, i.e. line shopping (Angelini et al. 2022; Wilkens 2021). Subagents trying to "out-predict" the market on travel, motivation or surface angles are unlikely to add value and may add noise. Keeping the ±3 pp adjustment cap and a default of 0 is consistent with the evidence.
- The Ramirez et al. correction is directly relevant: an **apparent edge driven by a few erroneous long odds** is a classic false positive. At soft Portuguese books, a price far above Pinnacle on a WTA/ITF outsider is more likely an error (a palpable-error void risk) or a stale price than true value.
- Lower tiers: softer prices but also greater information asymmetry (insiders, fixing; see section 6). This is the same mechanism that drives the stronger FLB there (Lahvička). A reasonable policy is **a higher EV threshold and a lower stake for ITF**, and extra caution with Challenger underdogs.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found that measures closing-line efficiency **separately for ATP vs WTA vs Challenger vs ITF** with effect sizes. Buchdahl's tier breakdown exists but its tables could not be read.
- No quantified evidence (betting-market level) was found for: Asian swing / jet lag, end-of-season motivation, defending points, surface transitions, indoor/outdoor, qualifiers/lucky losers, returning from injury.
- Koning's exact home-advantage effect size is unverified.

## 3. Retirements and walkovers: base rates by tour/level, predictors, betting impact

### Takeaway
Retirements are measured per 1,000 games. They are **higher in the lower tiers**, at about 1.56/1000 games for men and 1.36 for women in Challenger/ITF/WTA125, versus roughly 0.8-1.0/1000 for ATP tour events. They are rising over time: by at least 25% on the ATP and 50% on the WTA over 25 years. They are more common on hard and clay than on grass, and in the second set of best-of-three matches. Heat sharply increases heat-related medical events. Rigorous evidence on "a previous retirement predicts the next one" was not found. The betting impact depends on each bookmaker's settlement rule, which must be checked for Betclic, Betano, Placard and Solverde.

### Cited Findings
- **[STRONG] Lower tiers:** PLOS One (2024), retrospective cohort of **ATP Challenger, ITF Men's World Tennis Tour, WTA 125 and ITF Women's World Tennis Tour**. Overall incidence was **1.56 retirements per 1,000 games (men)** and **1.36 per 1,000 games (women)**, with **retirements increasing over the years**. Incidence was **higher on hard and clay than on grass**. Risk factors differed by sex: **tournament category was significant for men (ITF vs Challenger)** and **match round for women**. — [PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0304638); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38829827/); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11146734/)
- **[STRONG] Main tour:** Oliver et al. (2024), *European Journal of Sport Science*, "Retirements of professional tennis players in ATP and WTA tour events". **ATP:** Masters 1000 had the highest incidence, **1.03 per 1,000 games (95% CI 0.94-1.12)**, about **25% higher than Grand Slams**; **ATP 250 and 500 were about 5% higher than Slams**. **WTA:** Premier had **1.65/1000 (95% CI 1.48-1.83)** and Premier Mandatory **1.41/1000 (1.17-1.70)**, about **245% and 196% higher than Slams** respectively. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ejsc.12177); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11451576/)
- **[STRONG] Trend:** in the last 25 years retirements increased by **at least 25% on the ATP and 50% on the WTA** (university press release on the studies above). — [UVic](https://www.uvic.cat/en/news/retirements-from-matches-by-professional-tennis-players-have-increased-by-25-on-the-mens)
- **[MODERATE] Timing and walkovers:** ESPN analysis. In best-of-three, **the second set is the most common time to retire (39%** of retirements at major events over five years). **Walkovers have doubled at Grand Slams over 25 years**. ATP Grand Slam **withdrawals rose from 2.56% (1992) to 5.51% (2017)**. Another figure in the same search, "64 walkovers = 0.60% of 10,593 ATP matches", had an unclear period and attribution; treat it as unverified. — [ESPN](https://www.espn.com/tennis/story/_/id/21586057/tennis-failure-complete-numbers-rate-withdrawals-retirements-atp-tour)
- **[STRONG] Heat:** Smith et al. (2018), *Journal of Science and Medicine in Sport*: **360 Australian Open men's matches (first 4 rounds, 2014-2016)**. For each WBGT zone increase: **on-court doctor calls +47%, heat-related events +41%, cooling-device use +53%, post-match heat consults +87%**. Significant increases appear above **WBGT 28°C and 32°C**. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28919493/); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1440244017310241)
- **Betting rules:** retirement settlement rules differ by bookmaker. Common variants include voiding unless the match completes, settling if a set was completed, and settling on the player who advances. Rule pages exist for several books, but no Portuguese bookmaker rule was retrieved. — [Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/tennis/tennis-betting-rules-player-retires-rain-weather-delay); [RulesofSport](https://www.rulesofsport.com/betting/tennis/what-are-the-rules-on-retirements-when-betting-on-tennis/); [Outplayed](https://outplayed.com/blog/tennis-retirement-rules)

### Inferences
- Rough conversion (my estimate, not from a source): a best-of-three match averages about 20-24 games. At 1.56/1000 games, about **3% of Challenger/ITF men's matches** end in retirement. At about 0.8-1.0/1000, roughly **2% on the ATP tour**. The WTA non-Slam figure (1.4-1.65/1000) is closer to 3%.
- Retirement risk is systematically asymmetric: a player carrying an injury is priced shorter or longer depending on public information. If the soft book settles on "advances" or "1 set completed" while Pinnacle voids (or the reverse), **the fair-price comparison is not like-for-like**. The agent should record each book's rule, and the rules subagent should flag matches with injury doubts at books whose rule is unfavourable.
- Heat (Asian swing in late September/October, e.g. Chengdu/Wuhan) plausibly raises retirement risk. This is a reason to **avoid** backing a player with a known physical issue, not a reason to adjust probability upward for the opponent.

### Gaps
- No rigorous evidence retrieved on **predictors at the individual level**: previous retirement → next-match retirement, long previous match → retirement.
- No walkover base rates for Challenger/ITF.
- Settlement rules of **Betclic, Betano, Placard and Solverde** for tennis retirements were not retrieved and must be checked directly.
- No study measures whether markets *price* retirement risk correctly.

## 4. Line movement and bet timing: opening vs closing efficiency, steam, how much lines move

### Takeaway
The standard, evidence-backed view is that **Pinnacle's closing line is, on average, the most efficient tennis price**. Openers are posted at low limits and are moved by sharp money, which is why CLV is a valid skill metric. Tennis is somewhat less efficient than soccer, especially in the lower tiers. Some profitable tennis bettors show yields above what their CLV predicts, which suggests the close is not perfect in every niche. **No source retrieved quantifies typical movement from about 12 hours out to the start.** For a bettor who takes soft-book prices against the Pinnacle consensus, the evidence favours **betting as soon as a soft-book price exceeds the current fair price**, rather than waiting, and confirming just before the match.

### Cited Findings
- **[MODERATE] Opening vs closing dataset:** Buchdahl analysed **Pinnacle opening and closing odds for 68,361 matches (ATP, WTA, Challenger, ITF; May 2015-Jul 2019)**. He tested the FLB and efficiency and found **evidence of some inefficiency; tennis is "not as efficient as soccer"**. The explanations offered are **lower liquidity and the less-known Challenger/Futures players**. — [Pinnacle](https://www.pinnacle.com/betting-resources/en/tennis/how-efficient-is-the-tennis-betting-market/u522zlsepqja8q2n); [STN mirror](https://www.sportstradingnetwork.com/article/how-efficient-is-the-tennis-betting-market/)
- **[MODERATE] Closing line vs actual profit in tennis:** the case of tipster "Nishikori" (Pyckio). On **about 3,000 tennis bets**, the **actual ROI was +8.9%**, while the **expected ROI implied by Pinnacle closing lines was -0.2%**. He beat the close by about 3% on average, which is roughly break-even after margin. Buchdahl's view: Pinnacle closing lines are **on average** the most efficient, but that is compatible with some bettors selecting closing prices that are not "true". — [STN: "Do Pinnacle closing prices in Tennis tell the full story?"](https://www.sportstradingnetwork.com/article/do-pinnacle-closing-prices-in-tennis-tell-the-full-story-can-you-win-in-the-long-run-without-beating-them/); [Pyckio blog](https://blog.pyckio.com/en/pinnacle-closing-odds/)
- **[MODERATE] Market mechanics:** Pinnacle's traders post early lines at **reduced limits**. Much like an exchange, Pinnacle relies on sharp bettors to move the market, and **limits increase over time** towards the start. Movement at market makers (Pinnacle, Betfair) typically **precedes adjustments at other books**. — [Pinnacle: market movement](https://www.pinnacle.com/betting-resources/en/betting-strategy/market-movement-in-betting/4732xnzxpqpzrff5); [betstamp](https://www.betstamp.com/education/tennis-betting-strategy-guide)
- **[WEAK/opinion] When tennis lines move:** practitioners say the biggest tennis moves come **close to match time**, especially in the **last 24 hours**, and early sharp money often goes on **underdogs when openers are set mainly on rankings**. No figures are given. — [betstamp](https://www.betstamp.com/education/tennis-betting-strategy-guide); [bettinginsight](https://bettinginsight.net/tennis-betting/how-betting-lines-work-in-tennis-odds-spreads-and-key-markets/)
- **[STRONG] Data-snooping warning:** apparent timing or rule-based edges in tennis largely vanish under reality-check tests (Lyócsa & Výrost 2018, 40 rules). — [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00036846.2017.1394973)
- **[STRONG] Best-price effect:** profitable returns come from best-of-many-bookmakers odds, not from a single book (Angelini et al. 2022). This is consistent with **soft books lagging the sharp market**. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0377221721003234)
- Practitioner CLV logs exist, e.g. "My first 100 pre-match bets in tennis (comparing with Pinnacle closing line)" on Arbusers, but no figures were retrieved. — [Arbusers](https://arbusers.com/my-first-100-pre-match-bets-in-tennis-comparing-with-pinnacle-closing-line-t8067/)

### Inferences
- **Timing rule for this bettor:** the edge is soft-book staleness relative to Pinnacle. Staleness is largest right after Pinnacle moves and before soft books follow. So **bet as soon as the soft price is at or above the minimum odds**. Do not wait for "better" prices: the evidence says moves tend towards the truth, so waiting mostly erodes the edge.
- **Use Pinnacle's current price, not its opener, as "fair".** Pinnacle openers are low-limit and least efficient; the closer to the start, the more reliable the fair price. A fair price computed 12+ hours out should be **refreshed** before recommending. This matches the 60-minute odds-age filter; the pre-match check at T-40 min also captures an almost-closing line for CLV.
- **Direction of movement as a filter:** if Pinnacle has moved *against* the selection since the plan (the price has lengthened), the "value" at the soft book may be a stale price on the wrong side of sharp money. The agent should re-price and usually cancel. If Pinnacle has shortened *towards* the selection, the bet is likely already +CLV.
- **ITF/Challenger steam on an outsider** is ambiguous: it can be sharp modelling, insider injury information, or fixing (section 6). The rational response for a price-first bettor is **not to follow it and not to bet against it** (skip the match).
- Because some bettors (e.g. Nishikori) outperform their CLV, the close is imperfect in niches. But the regra de paragem (stop rule) based on CLV remains the right primary metric at small samples.

### Gaps
- **No quantitative source was retrieved on the typical size of tennis line moves** (e.g. median % change in implied probability from T-12h to close, by tier).
- Buchdahl's per-tier comparison of opening vs closing FLB, and the returns from betting openers vs closers, could not be read.
- No peer-reviewed tennis study of "steam" profitability or the lag of soft books behind Pinnacle was found.
- How quickly Portuguese soft books (Betclic, Betano, Placard, Solverde) follow Pinnacle is unknown. The agent's own logs could measure this.

## 5. Fatigue effects: previous match duration, matches in the last N days, and whether markets price it

### Takeaway
Fatigue clearly affects **within-match** performance (serve, net, running decline in later sets). But **evidence that fatigue from the previous match reduces next-match win probability beyond what the market prices is weak**. The best-known betting analysis (Pinnacle, Grand Slam QF-F) found **+3.7% ROI on 76 bets** for the fresher player, which is not statistically meaningful. An amateur study of 481 matchups found no significant effect. **No rigorous study with effect sizes on "minutes played in last N days" versus market odds was found**, including from Kovalchik.

### Cited Findings
- **[WEAK] Pinnacle, "Analysing fatigue at Grand Slams":** blind-backing the **fresher player** (less court time) in **QF, SF and F** would have won **48 of 76 bets (63.16%)** for **ROI +3.70%** (a £100 stake per bet returned £281). The **11 players with more than 4 hours less court time** than their opponent returned **+10.45% ROI**. The author concedes this is a **small sample**. — [Pinnacle](https://www.pinnacle.com/betting-resources/en/tennis/analysing-fatigue-at-grand-slams/m3djq42thz47zk4n)
- **[WEAK] Amateur study (blog) using Sackmann data:** **481 matchups from 2016**. Fatigue did **not significantly alter win/loss in the next match**. — [sportsbrain blog](https://beyondtheaverage.wordpress.com/2017/10/27/is-fatigue-a-factor-tennis-in-tennis/)
- **[MODERATE] Within-match fatigue:** in Grand Slam men's five-set matches, **serve, net and running-related performance declined** and return/winner-related performance increased in later sets. — [PMC 7386150](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7386150/); see also [PMC 3995216](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3995216/)
- **[WEAK/opinion]** Some practitioner content asserts that "schedule and fatigue are underpriced by betting markets more often than almost any other variable". No data was offered. — appeared in search summary with the Pinnacle fatigue article; unattributed
- Kovalchik's retrieved work covers win prediction (2016), match duration by format (Kovalchik & Ingram 2018) and in-match forecasting. **No Kovalchik paper on previous-match fatigue and win probability was found.** — [ResearchGate: duration](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/323461166_Estimating_the_duration_of_professional_tennis_matches_for_varying_formats); [ResearchGate: GOAT](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/301577517_Searching_for_the_GOAT_of_tennis_win_prediction)

### Inferences
- Fatigue should **not** be used as a routine reason to push probability up by 1-3 pp. The evidence does not show the market under-prices it at 250/Challenger level. At most, it is a **reason to skip** (e.g. a player coming off a 3h+ match the night before, playing early the next day in heat), which fits the "subagents as filters" design.
- The Grand Slam "fresher player" effect (if real) concerns best-of-five and late rounds. It is not directly transferable to best-of-three Asian swing events.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study quantifying the effect of previous-match minutes or sets, or of matches played in the last 3/7 days, on next-match win probability **relative to closing odds**.
- No study on back-to-back days (common in Challengers/ITF, e.g. qualifying then main draw) versus market pricing.

## 6. Match-fixing risk in low tiers (ITIA/IBIA): suspicious line movements and how to treat them

### Takeaway
Tennis is one of the two sports with the most suspicious-betting alerts: **74 of 300 IBIA alerts in 2025 (25%)**, and in Q3 2025 it briefly overtook football at about 30% of cases. Alerts and sanctions are **concentrated in lower tiers (ITF World Tennis Tour and Challengers)**, and **umpires** as well as players are sanctioned. Academic work shows that fixed matches are detectable as **large, "irrational" moves away from model prices**. For a price-first bettor this means that in ITF/Challenger, a soft-book price that looks like value against a *moving* sharp line may be the wrong side of informed money. The rational policy is **avoidance** of suspicious markets rather than trying to profit from them.

### Cited Findings
- **[STRONG, official] IBIA 2025 annual report:** **300 suspicious betting alerts across all sports in 2025 (+29% y/y)**. Football had **110** and **tennis 74 (25%)**; football and tennis together were over 60% of alerts. In 2025 IBIA data contributed to **54 matches confirmed as corrupted** across sports. In tennis, **10 players and 6 umpires** were sanctioned. — [Yogonet](https://www.yogonet.com/international/news/2026/02/04/117454-ibia-reports-300-suspicious-betting-alerts-in-2025-up-29-); [Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/300-suspicious-betting-alerts-reported-to-ibia-in-2025); [NEXT.io](https://next.io/news/betting/ibia-suspicious-betting-alerts-on-the-rise/)
- **[MODERATE] IBIA Q3 2025:** tennis produced the **most suspicious cases in Q3 2025 (about 30% vs about 25% football)**, overtaking football for the first time that year. **The majority of tennis cases came from lower-tier competitions**; the "fragmented tournament structures and limited visibility at ITF level" are cited. — [Tribuna](https://tribuna.com/en/betting/news/2025-12-05-tennis-overtakes-football-in-suspicious-betting-alerts-ibia-reports/)
- **[STRONG, official] ITIA match alerts:** **2024: 95 alerts** (Q1 25, Q2 19, Q3 28, Q4 23). **2025: Q1 9, Q2 10, Q4 23** (Q3 not retrieved). Q2 2025 alerts spanned **ATP Challenger, WTA and World Tennis Tour** events. One search summary also said 34 people were sanctioned in 2024 for match-fixing and related offences; this is unverified. — [ITIA Q4 2024](https://www.itia.tennis/news/itia-news/itia-quarterly-update-q4-2024/); [ITIA Annual Review 2024 PDF](https://www.itia.tennis/media/ommjl12w/itia-annual-review-2024.pdf); [ITIA Q1 2025](https://www.itia.tennis/news/itia-news/itia-quarterly-update-q1-2025/); [ITIA Q4 2025](https://www.itia.tennis/news/itia-news/itia-quarterly-update-q4-2025/); [ITIA Annual Review 2025](https://www.itia.tennis/news/itia-news/itia-annual-review-2025/) ([PDF](https://www.itia.tennis/media/fohfcuoy/itia_annualreview2025_final.pdf)); [Tennisbettingforum summary](https://tennisbettingforum.com/tennis-betting-integrity/)
- **[OFFICIAL] What triggers an alert:** unusual patterns such as **large amounts placed on outsiders, bets from new accounts, and clusters of similar bets**. — [ITIA: What is a betting alert?](https://www.itia.tennis/news/itia-news/what-is-a-betting-alert/)
- **[STRONG] Detection by odds divergence:** Forrest & McHale (2019), *IMA Journal of Management Mathematics* 30(4):431: **in-play forecasting models are compared in real time with market odds worldwide**, and suspicion arises where they diverge. This forensic evidence has been **accepted as primary evidence at the Court of Arbitration for Sport**. — [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/imaman/article-abstract/30/4/431/5522261); [Liverpool repository PDF](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3050029/1/Using%20Statistics%20to%20Detect%20Match%20Fixing%20in%20Sport%20revision,%2023.4.19.pdf)
- **[MODERATE] Rodenberg & Feustel (2014),** *Journal of Prediction Markets*: using Elo and common-opponent models as fair prices, they find prima facie evidence of **about 23 matches per year** likely manipulated. **Large irrational pre-match moves away from model prices are indicative of fixing**, and effort is lower in first rounds. — [JPM](https://www.ubplj.org/index.php/jpm/article/view/866); [SSRN](https://www.ssrn.com/abstract=2507959); review: [Int. Sports Law Journal](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40318-016-0096-x)
- Further methods: Hatfield (2019), PhD thesis, "Statistical Methods for Detecting Match-Fixing in Tennis" (Lancaster). Winkelmann et al. (2026), arXiv, model in-play betting volumes to flag anomalies (Serie B data, not tennis). — [Lancaster PDF](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/141874/1/2019hatfieldphd.pdf); [arXiv 2605.30209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30209)
- **Link to the FLB:** Lahvička's evidence that the tennis FLB reflects **bookmakers protecting themselves against insiders** is the market-pricing footprint of this information asymmetry, and it is stronger in lower-ranked matches. — [MPRA](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/47905/1/MPRA_paper_47905.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Practical rules** (inferred from the evidence above, not tested):
  1. In **ITF (M15/M25/W15/W35)**, require a higher EV (or exclude) and cap stakes lower. This is where alerts and sanctions concentrate and where information is thinnest.
  2. **Skip any match where the Pinnacle/consensus price has moved sharply towards an outsider without public news** (injury, withdrawal). That is the signature used by integrity monitors. Do not back the side the money is on, and do not take the "value" on the side it left.
  3. A large gap between one soft book and the consensus in a low-tier match is more likely a **stale price after informed money** or a **palpable error** than value.
  4. Recommendations on matches later reported as suspicious or voided should be logged to see whether they cluster in the ITF segment.
- Because 6 umpires were sanctioned in 2025, in-play markets at ITF level (live score feed integrity) carry extra risk. This supports keeping live entries off ITF.

### Gaps
- The ITIA 2025 annual review PDF could not be read. Full-year 2025 ITIA alert counts and the **split of alerts/sanctions by tier (ITF vs Challenger vs tour)** are missing.
- No quantitative rule of the form "a move of X% in Y minutes indicates fixing" is available publicly. Integrity monitors' thresholds are proprietary.
- No study quantifies the betting loss of a price-first bettor who ignores integrity signals in ITF markets.
